Why news matters

A child who knows the world grows up ready for it.

Every child starts at the centre of their own small world. A few calm, true stories a day gently widen the circle: from their street, to their country, to the planet they share with eight billion people.

01

Words that keep growing

Every story carries a few new words. Tap one, understand it, save it. Vocabulary builds quietly, one morning at a time.

02

Questions, not scrolling

Real events spark real curiosity: why did it happen, where is that, what would I do? Each story ends with a question to think about.

03

Kindness that travels

Reading about children in other places helps kids care about people beyond their own street, and see how much they share.

04

Telling true from false

In a UK study, only about 2 in every 100 children sorted every real news story from the fakes. Kids who meet real, checked news early learn what trustworthy looks like.National Literacy Trust, Commission on Fake News, 2018

05

A voice at the table

Knowing what is happening gives children the confidence to join the conversation at dinner, in class, and with friends.

06

How the world works

History, geography, civics and science, woven into today's stories. Lessons stop being chapters and start being real life.

How it works

The news in a few minutes, not a never-ending feed.

FFMPA is built like a morning paper. A short edition arrives, your child reads it, and then it's finished. There is nothing to scroll forever.

1

Two fresh editions

A morning and an evening edition in your local time, with only news from the last 3 days.

2

Swipe, one story at a time

Each story fits on one screen. Swipe up for the next, tap More for why it matters and a question to think about.

3

Learn the words behind the news

Tap any underlined person, place or idea for a quick explainer, and save it to a personal word bank.

Three reading levels

The same story, told for their age.

Every story comes at three levels, so a seven-year-old, an eleven-year-old and a fourteen-year-old can read the same news in the way that suits them.

Beyond the headlines

News that explains itself.

Reading what happened is the start. FFMPA also shows why it happened, lets children follow a story through the week, and gives them something to do with what they learned.

Hard ideas, drawn by hand

When a story needs a concept made clear, a sketch draws itself in three steps: why smog gets trapped over a city, how a satellite stays up, why the planet is warming. One line of text per step, written for each reading level.

  • Opens from the story, right where the question comes up
  • Three steps, about a minute
  • Reused whenever the idea returns in the news

For classrooms and young writers

Read it together. Then write your own.

Classroom mode

A weekly class pack, built from the news

A teacher names a class and gets a six-character code. Each week brings a five-question quiz for the big screen, a printable worksheet with an answer key and one question to discuss.

  • No pupil names, emails or accounts
  • Teachers see class totals only, never one child
  • Children join with a grown-up's PIN
Young reporters

One assignment a week, written by children

Who did what, and where? Children write a headline and a few sentences about something they saw. The best appear on Sunday's front page.

  • A grown-up's PIN is needed to send a report
  • An editor reads every report before it appears
  • Shown with age and country only, never a name

Safe by design

Real news, without the scary parts.

Every story is written from trusted reports and checked by a human editor before it goes out. Our standards are public.

Strict language rules

No swearing, slurs, graphic detail, fear words or sensational headlines. A blocked word list and editor review catch anything that slips.

Facts checked twice

Every fact appears in at least two reputable sources, and grown-ups can see the sources under every story.

Fair on every side

We explain how things work and what happened. We never tell children what to think about politics or belief.

No tracking, no profiling

No ads based on your child, no selling of data, no comments, chat or strangers. Sponsor cards, when shown, are approved by our team.

No links out

Nothing in the app links to other websites. Photos are free-to-use images, credited in plain text. No AI images of real people.

Hard topics handled with care

War stories only for older readers and only if you allow them. Disasters focus on helpers and safety.

Questions

Good to know

Who writes the stories?

Each edition is drafted from trusted news reports, written at three reading levels, and then read, corrected and approved by a human editor before it is published.

Why only the last 3 days of news?

So nothing feels stale. Old stories disappear automatically, and each edition only adds what is new since the last one.

What do you need to know about my child?

Only an optional first name or nickname, a reading level and a country. No birthday, school, photo or contact details. See the privacy policy for details.

Can my child end up on another website?

No. FFMPA never links out: sources and photo credits are shown as plain text, sponsor cards carry no links, and sharing only copies a link to the story for a grown-up to send.

Can schools use FFMPA?

Yes. Classroom mode gives a teacher a class code, a weekly quiz for the big screen and a printable worksheet. It holds no pupil names, and teachers see class totals only.

What happens to what my child writes?

A Young reporters piece is sent only after a grown-up enters the PIN. An editor reads it before it can appear, and it is shown with age and country only. Private headlines a child writes for fun stay on the device.

Is it in other languages?

FFMPA is in English for now.

Are there ads?

There are no ads based on your child, and we never sell data. Some editions may show a clearly labelled sponsor card that our team has approved, the same for every child in a country. They are off by default for the youngest readers.
